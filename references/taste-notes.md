# Taste notes — what good frames do, seen and judged

Written 2026-11-17 after studying ~50 high-quality product-film samples (video-shotcraft's motion samples, every
category) frame by frame, to train the eye rather than to collect moves. These are observations turned into a
designer's habits, with this skill's own judgement on what to take and what not. They apply to every film, explainers
included. Techniques by name live in `promo-shotcraft.md`; this file is about seeing.

## What the eye notices first
- **Scale contrast is the hierarchy.** One element is huge (a word filling half the width, a number taller than the
  UI around it) and everything else is small and quiet. Mid-sized everything is the look of a template. Ask of every
  frame: which ONE thing is big, and is it really big?
- **No running labels: a film is not a slide deck.** No section label in a corner ("02 · DELIVERY"), no brand tag
  top-right, no page count, no progress ticks repeated over the whole film; the user rejected them on sight
  (2026-11-17: "左上角和右上角的文字没有必要，这也不是ppt"). Continuity comes from the world, the light and the
  recurring subject, not from print furniture. Text on screen earns its place in THIS moment: the caption, a word the
  picture needs, a legal line where it is required (an unofficial film says so at the open and the close, not in
  every frame).
- **One accent, used as meaning.** A ground (near-black with a soft glow, or a pale haze), ink, and ONE colour that
  marks what matters now. A second colour only for a second meaning.
- **Empty space is composed.** The best frames are mostly empty and still feel full, because the one subject has a
  clear place in a consistent grid. Edges matter: margins are generous and identical across shots.
- **Type has a voice.** A confident grotesk at large sizes, tight tracking; one word set in an italic serif (or the
  accent colour) for the human beat ("That's a *wrap*."). Two families at most, used the same way all film.

## Depth and light
- **Every frame has depth, even a flat one.** A soft radial glow behind the subject; blurred props in the foreground
  and background (out-of-focus tags, cards, particles) with a sharp focal plane between them; a real contact shadow
  under anything that floats. Flat-on-flat reads as a slide.
- **Light is placed, not sprayed.** A spotlight finds the subject; a single sheen crosses the hero once; a coloured
  glow sits behind glass. On white, brightening is invisible: to use light, go dark.
- **Objects get a stage.** A soft studio sweep with a floor shadow, a dark brushed surface, a navy grid floor. The
  subject is placed ON something, at an angle, not pasted IN a rectangle.

## Motion, seen in single frames
- **Motion blur sells speed** where it is fast (a dial sweeping, digits rolling, a whip), and everything lands sharp.
- **Things arrive from blur into focus**; text surfaces rather than slides.
- **Bold crops are confidence.** A word or a UI cut by the frame edge during a move reads as cinematic; showing the
  whole screen every time reads as documentation.
- **A whole-frame transformation is a moment**: a grey field flooding with colour in a wave, the ground slamming from
  light to dark, a chapter's colour pushing the last one out. One or two per film, at turns in the story.
- **Numbers are characters**: they roll like an odometer, overshoot a ceiling, settle on a sourced value, and get the
  biggest type on screen.

## Structure, seen across a film
- **Chapters can be colours.** A full-bleed saturated ground per chapter (one headline, one small card) gives a long
  film a pulse a viewer can feel without reading.
- **The close is a lockup**, not a fade: mark, wordmark, a letter-spaced small-caps line, held still.

## What not to take
- **A house style.** Those samples all promote one product in one clean SaaS look; consistent, and narrow. This skill
  makes films about cells, cities, cats and products: take the DISCIPLINE (scale contrast, one accent,
  depth, placed light, holds), and let each subject choose its own look (`video-looks.md`).
- **Decoration without a reason.** Every ornament (hairline circles, tick rings, metadata) must belong to the subject
  or the claim; ornament for its own sake is the AI look.
- **Spectacle over truth.** A move that looks great but shows something false is worse than a plain true one.

## A designer's pass on any frame (ten seconds each)
1. What is the ONE big thing? Is it big enough?
2. Is there depth (glow, focus planes, shadow)?
3. Is the light placed, and only once?
4. Is there any text on the frame that is not doing a job right now (a corner label, a tag)? Remove it.
5. Would this frame make a good poster on its own?
